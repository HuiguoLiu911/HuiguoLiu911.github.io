The question

Crypto dashboards are full of “signals”: momentum, volume spikes, open interest, funding rates, news sentiment. Paid data vendors sell more of them. Before paying for any of it, I wanted a straight answer to one question: do these signals predict BTC, ETH and SOL returns well enough, out of sample and after costs, to be worth acting on?

So I built a research system rather than a trading bot. It never places real orders and never touches exchange account keys.

Data

  • Price and volume: 12 months of hourly Binance spot candles for each asset, 8,761 rows per asset with zero missing hours, zero duplicates and no misaligned timestamps. CoinGecko is a degraded fallback that can never overwrite Binance rows.
  • Derivatives: about 60 days of OKX open interest and about 100 days of funding-rate settlements, all from free public endpoints.
  • News: RSS headlines with publication time and first-seen time stored separately, so a headline cannot be used before the system actually saw it.

Everything lands in DuckDB with one rule that never bends: a candle’s timestamp is its open time in UTC, only closed candles are stored, and missing hours stay missing instead of being interpolated.

Method

The pipeline runs collection, features, labels, signal research, backtesting, model validation, transparent scoring, forward paper trading and a Chinese-language Streamlit dashboard.

Most of the work went into making the evaluation hard to fool:

  • Features use only data available at decision time; labels live in a separate module and never enter a feature frame.
  • Model training uses expanding windows with purged walk-forward splits, so no training label overlaps the validation period; imputation and scaling are fit on training data only.
  • The backtest enters at the next hour’s open with adverse slippage and fees on both sides, one position per asset, and compares every rule against buy-and-hold, always-long, momentum and 20 seeded random-entry baselines.
  • Ablations add one source at a time (price only, then volume, open interest, funding) to see whether each new input earns its place.
  • A second-pass integrity review checked timestamps, leakage, fills and paper-trade ordering, and 94 automated tests cover the edge cases.

Results

  • Over the 12-month price and volume panel (240-day walk-forward), momentum-plus-volume returned -33.7% and the price-only XGBoost model -43.9%, against -21.4% for simply holding the three assets.
  • On the shorter panel where derivatives data exists (28-day walk-forward), the best rule used volume only (+7.2%) and adding funding to the model gave +5.4%, both close to buy-and-hold at +7.6%.
  • Adding open interest and funding did not improve probability calibration consistently.

The verdict the system writes for itself is MAYBE: free data is enough to test the idea, not enough to justify buying paid data.

What it changed

The answer was “don’t spend money yet”, and the project says so in its first line. Instead of a paid-API upgrade, the next step is a prespecified evidence plan: collect 120 more days of continuous open interest to reach 180 days, and paper-trade one fixed rule for at least 90 days or 100 closed trades before revisiting the decision.

Next steps

  • Collect 120 more days of continuous open-interest data to reach at least 180 days.
  • Paper-trade one fixed rule for at least 90 days or 100 closed trades.
  • Revisit the paid-data decision only when out-of-sample results beat the baselines.
