Call the members who can still be won back

YMCA retention concept · monthly churn-risk model and uplift-targeted outreach test

  • churn modeling
  • uplift
  • experiment design

Risk-ranked call lists sent staff to members already gone; a monthly churn model plus a randomized pilot shows outreach pays off mainly in the mid-risk band.

Real

In Summer 2026 a Carlson Analytics Lab team consolidated YMCA of the North's two membership systems in DuckDB (539K members, 9.4M monthly engagement records, ID match 50% to 79%), defined churn with a 40-day grace period, found four behavior segments with 60% to 89% annual retention, built a first-60-day churn model at AUC about 0.64 and started a next-60-day member-month snapshot dataset, after the client asked for operational outreach decisions rather than a higher AUC.

Business context

Most membership revenue at a YMCA is lost quietly: a member stops coming, and a few weeks later the monthly draft stops too. Branch staff already make retention calls, but in this scenario from whatever list someone pulls that week, usually “hasn’t visited in a month”. The data covers 539K members from two membership systems and 9.4M monthly engagement records. Churn means a membership ends and is not renewed within a 40-day grace period.

Branch membership directors own the calls and the staff hours; the membership lead owns the retention target. The earlier engagement gave them segments and a first-60-day churn model. Their next question was operational: each month, who should we contact, and is it worth the time?

The problem

The first model scored each new member once, at day 60, with a validation AUC of about 0.64. It says nothing about the established member whose visits drop from three a week to one, and nobody knew whether a call changes anything. A list ranked by churn risk answers “who is leaving”. Staff need “who would stay if we called”.

  • North star: 90-day retention among outreach-eligible members: the share active at a monthly snapshot who still hold a membership, or rejoined within the grace, 90 days later.
  • Guardrail 1, staff hours: outreach time logged in the CRM per branch per week; budget 9 hours.
  • Guardrail 2, opt-outs: share of members who unsubscribe or set do-not-contact within 30 days of assignment; ceiling 2%.
  • Guardrail 3, complaints: complaints that mention outreach, per 10,000 members contacted; reviewed weekly, stop at 10.

What I built

I built a monthly next-60-day churn model on member-month snapshots and a randomized pilot to find where outreach moves retention.

I rejected two alternatives. Extending the first-60-day model would still score each member once. A discrete-time survival model handles censoring better, but staff work in monthly batches, and a calibrated “chance of lapsing in the next 60 days” maps straight onto a monthly queue. I also tried an individual uplift model (T-learner) on the pilot data and dropped it: with 28,000 members, its Qini curve on a held-out half was indistinguishable from random, so targeting uses risk bands.

On the first of each month a DuckDB job reads both membership extracts and the visit logs, applies the ID crosswalk, and writes one row per active member with about 40 features computed strictly before the snapshot date. Targeting then applies the band policy, a 90-day no-recontact window, opt-out flags and a per-branch staff-hours cap. The Power BI ops view gives each branch its queue with reason codes (“visits down 60% vs usual”) and holds only member keys; contact details stay in the CRM. Staff can skip anyone with a reason (9% skipped in the pilot).

The simulated pilot ran at 12 branches over two monthly scoring runs and an 8-week outreach window. Eligible members sat in deciles 4–10, had no outreach in the prior 90 days and no opt-out, and could enter once. I sampled 4,000 per decile and randomized 1:1 within decile × branch, giving 14,000 per arm. Treatment was a personal branch email plus one call attempt; control got the standard newsletter. At a baseline 90-day retention of about 87%, an MDE of 1.2 pp with α = 0.05 and power 0.8 needs about 11,800 per arm. Outcomes were read once, after every member’s 90 days matured; guardrails were checked weekly against stop rules (opt-outs above 3% at any branch, or over 15 hours per branch per week two weeks running). I pre-registered two tests: the pooled effect, and mid band (D5–D8) minus top band (D9–D10).

Monthly AUC ranged from 0.71 to 0.78. The three lowest months fall around the 2025 system migration; outside them, AUC drifts down slowly. Isotonic calibration cut expected calibration error from 1.9 pp to 0.4 pp, and the top decile predicted 22.6% churn against 21.8% observed. The first model’s 0.64 used a different label and population, so the visit-recency rule is the fair comparison.

Pilot. Reach was partial: 31% of calls connected, 48% of emails were opened, and 63% of treated members had at least one touch. All effects are intention-to-treat.

  • Pooled, D4–D10 (14,000 per arm): 90-day retention 87.2% in control, +1.1 pp [0.3, 1.9], below the 1.2 pp I powered for.
  • Mid band, D5–D8 (8,000 per arm): 91.1% control, +1.8 pp [0.9, 2.7], about a 20% relative cut in churn.
  • Top band, D9–D10 (4,000 per arm): 75.2% control, +0.1 pp [−1.8, 2.0]. D10 alone: −0.4 pp [−3.3, 2.5].
  • Contrast, mid minus top: 1.7 pp [−0.4, 3.8], p ≈ 0.11. The pilot was sized for the pooled effect; its MDE for this contrast was about 3 pp.

The profiles explain the curve. In D5–D8, 64% were lapsing regulars: a steady habit, visits down by half or more against their own three-month baseline, still coming in. In D10, 71% had not visited for six weeks or more. The mid-band gap was 2.9 pp at day 30 and 1.8 pp at day 90, so part of the effect fades.

Two guardrails moved the wrong way. Staff time averaged 10.9 hours per branch per week against a budget of 9, and that was my sizing error: 1,167 treated members per branch over eight weeks is 146 a week, and at a logged 4.5 minutes each (a third of it CRM logging) the budget could not hold. Opt-outs were 1.4% in treatment versus 0.5% in control, +0.9 pp [0.7, 1.1]: inside the ceiling overall, but 2.6% in D10. Complaints: 11 across 14,000 treated members, about 8 per 10,000.

Business impact

The decision: fill each branch’s call queue from D5–D8, lapsing regulars first, and stop calling the top decile. One outreach assignment costs about $1.82 (4.5 staff minutes at a $24 loaded rate, plus email). Per 1,000 members assigned, the mid band yields about 18 extra retained members, or $101 each (CI roughly $67–$202). A risk-ranked D8–D10 list yields about 6, at $303 each; blanket outreach to D4–D10 yields 11, at $165. I value a retained member at three months of illustrative $65 dues, $195. That is gross revenue and ignores later months, so it ranks the options rather than proving ROI.

The contrast itself is not significant, so the call rests on what is: the mid-band effect and the cost gap. If the bands are truly equal, moving hours to D5–D8 loses nothing; if the estimates hold, it triples retained members per staff hour. At about 500 assignments per branch a month (9 hours a week at 4.5 minutes), that is roughly 9 extra retained members per branch per month instead of 3, counted directly in the north star. The top decile would get an automated win-back email instead. It is untested and D10 had the highest opt-out rate, so it ships with its own holdout. Before scaling I would run a confirmatory wave sized for the contrast (about 36,000 per arm, roughly 70 branch-months at the 9-hour cap), keep a permanent 10% holdout, and read 180-day retention.

Technical deep dive

Data & pipeline

One row per active member per month from February 2022: about 7.0M member-months, roughly 160K a month. Features include visits in the last 30/60/90 days, last-30-day visits relative to the member’s own prior 90 days, weeks since last visit, program enrollments, membership type, tenure, and months to annual renewal. Gender, ethnicity and ZIP stay out of the model; age band is used only for fairness checks.

Model design

Baseline: logistic regression with splines on the visit features. Candidate: LightGBM (depth 6, early stopping on a member-grouped slice of the training months). No resampling, because capacity planning reads the probabilities directly; I evaluate on PR-AUC and fit isotonic calibration on the validation months.

Metric definitions

Label: the membership ends within 60 days of the snapshot and is not renewed within the 40-day grace. Uplift is treatment minus control 90-day retention, with Wald 95% intervals computed within deciles and combined.

Evaluation

Time split with three skipped snapshot months between blocks, so every label (60 days plus the grace) closes before the next block starts: train February 2022–March 2023, validate July–September 2023, test January 2024 onward. Hyperparameters were tuned with member-grouped folds inside the training months.

Failure cases

  • An early version reached AUC 0.91. The cause was a has-end-date flag: the CRM writes end dates for monthly memberships only after cancellation, so the feature encoded the label. I removed it and now treat any AUC above 0.85 as a leakage alarm.
  • Around the 2025 system migration, members reissued under new IDs looked like churners, and AUC in those three months fell to 0.71–0.73. Unmatched-ID members are now excluded from queues.
  • Seasonal members (summer-only swimmers) land in D9–D10 every spring and come back anyway, inflating the high-risk, no-lift group.

What I’d optimize next

Separate call and email arms, since calls drive almost all the cost; a causal-forest uplift model once two randomized waves exist; and allocation that maximizes expected retained members per staff hour under the cap.

If I made this real

  • Rebuild the snapshot on de-identified tables and publish the real AUC, calibration curve and decile table against the visit-recency rule.
  • Agree the pilot with branch directors and pass a privacy review of contact and opt-out rules before anyone is contacted.
  • Time real calls and CRM logging to replace the 4.5-minute figure, and confirm the dues mix, including financially assisted memberships.
  • Size the live pilot for the mid-versus-top contrast, which at a 1.7 pp gap means about three times the simulated mid and top cells.
  • Release the pipeline and analysis code with a synthetic member-month dataset for review without client data.
