The question

Analytics student teams, and client-facing analytics teams generally, leave first sponsor meetings with messy notes, unclear KPIs, vague data requests and ambiguous next steps. Having just been through a sponsor kickoff at the Carlson Analytics Lab, I wanted to know whether a GenAI copilot could turn that ambiguity into a structured kickoff package in minutes, without inventing commitments the sponsor never made.

Data

The app takes current meeting notes or a transcript, optional previous meeting notes, and optional anonymized or synthetic project data. The repository contains only three synthetic sample inputs and one sample output report. No client notes or data were used; the memo’s position is that project data context should be anonymized or synthetic, never raw client datasets.

Method

I built the proof of concept in Streamlit over Azure AI Foundry / Azure OpenAI chat and embedding deployments:

  1. Chunk the three context sources and embed them. Retrieval is a weighted cosine similarity over the chunks: current notes are authoritative, previous notes get a lower weight as historical context, and synthetic data gets a lower weight as directional only.
  2. Several short structured-JSON chat calls generate the package: a business problem summary, a project charter with in-scope and out-of-scope sections and open questions, a KPI priority matrix on an urgency-by-feasibility grid with four quadrants, a traffic-light data request checklist, one sponsor follow-up email using the user’s sender, tone and meeting-date settings, multiple action plan options, and a Jira-style sprint board of action items plus a next-meeting agenda.
  3. Rule-based guardrails check for invalid evidence citations, overpromising language, missing required sections, a missing email and missing action plan options. If anything fires, the warnings and the draft go back to the model for one conservative repair rewrite; remaining warnings are still shown to the user.
  4. The result is exported as a markdown intake report.

Results

  • One click on the sample notes produces the full seven-part package across five review tabs (Summary, Charter, KPI & Data, Email & Actions, Guardrails & Export).
  • The guardrail and repair design means unsupported commitments are flagged or rewritten rather than silently passed through, and requested data that may not exist is marked as a question, not an asset.
  • The project memo sets pilot targets for 5–10 teams: charter drafting under 10 minutes instead of 45–60, at least 80% of action items usable without major edits, at least 75% of KPI placements and data rankings judged relevant, and zero invented sponsor commitments or data assets.

What it changed

The deliverable was the running app, the project memo (hypothesis, pilot, quality, cost, scale, risks, assumptions) and the sample report.

Limitations and next steps

The pilot metrics are targets, not measured results; no user study was run. Retrieval is a simple weighted cosine over chunks rather than a vector database, which is fine for a few pages of notes but not for long transcript histories. Meeting notes can contain sensitive client information, so a real deployment needs authentication, no server-side storage by default and redaction. Next steps: run the pilot with student teams, add project workspaces with persistent sponsor history, and integrate with Teams or a project management tool.
